# Audiovisual direction

These are creative decision criteria and proposed starting points, not measured
Apple production settings. Choose gestures for their meaning in this film; a
promo is not required to use every technique listed here.

## Composition and attention

Start with what the audience should want or understand about the subject. Show
evidence of that benefit through material appropriate to the brief: an action in
an interface, a product in use, footage, a transformation, code becoming a visible
result, or typography where the words themselves carry the demonstration. Strong
headlines support the sales argument; empty slogans cannot substitute for it.

For a before/after concept, preserve enough of the same content or subject that
the improvement is visible. Let the changes in composition, motion, color, and
sound supply the proof. Choose this structure when it fits the user's concept,
not as a mandatory formula for every video.

Give each shot one dominant idea and an unmistakable focal point. Several objects
can support that idea. Compose for the delivery frame, with breathing room and
intentional negative space. A giant word, detailed material close-up, or tiny
object surrounded by space can each be the hero.

Design hierarchy over time, not just within a poster. If an interrupting symbol,
a headline, and a product each need the viewer's attention, assign distinct beats
or a deliberate transfer of focus. Show separate keyframes for those beats rather
than shrinking them into a single crowded composition. Keep storyboard labels
and production notes outside the actual video frame.

Every visible line, ring, panel, and secondary label needs an identifiable job:
carry information, expose a real relationship, or become part of the transition.
Remove decoration whose purpose cannot be demonstrated. Product names, runtime
labels, code windows, and closing subtitles are content decisions, not obligatory
elements of an Apple-inspired frame.

Use changing scale to build discovery: detail, silhouette, full object, benefit.
For software, focus on the interaction or result being demonstrated, then reveal
the surrounding interface when it helps orientation. Give real product evidence
enough screen time to register.

## Typography as performance

Write short, concrete phrases; choose line breaks as part of the composition.
Establish a stable hierarchy for hero words, supporting copy, and necessary detail.
Pick a font with the required language coverage and usable rights. Apple-like
direction does not require Apple fonts or branded assets.

Make a word's behavior reinforce its meaning: expand, connect, reveal, move,
transform. Useful treatments include:

- **Scale takeover:** one word expands beyond the frame and becomes the next
  scene's field. Preserve a readable moment before the crop.
- **Mask reveal:** a word appears through a precisely aligned edge; that edge
  can become the following shot's boundary.
- **Selective glow or color:** energy belongs to a keyword or moment, with a
  quieter surrounding field and a clean readable resting state.
- **Typographic handoff:** a shared word or letter retains position while the
  rest of the sentence transforms around it.
- **Moving color:** an animated gradient travels through a hero word while its
  letters shift, stretch, or sway, then settle into readable spacing.
- **Letter choreography:** a staggered impact, sideways fall and recovery, or
  elastic reassembly gives a key word its own action and payoff.

When a word owns the frame, give it a deliberate performance beyond a routine
entrance. Propose a treatment that suits its meaning and the desired energy.
The examples above are options, not a required sequence or a preset to repeat.

Verify final rendered font metrics and line breaks. Size and hold text for the
actual viewing context, not just a large editing monitor.

## Motion and transitions

Describe each gesture's starting state, path, acceleration, arrival, and hold.
Use decisive entrances, controlled settling, and contrast between active passages
and stillness. Rapid cuts, elastic motion, or complex effects can be appropriate
when the concept supports them; refinement does not mean constant slowness.

### Images and products must participate in the motion

Give every featured photo, image, or product an authored motion treatment with
perceptible impact at delivery size and normal speed. Define the reveal, the
change that earns attention, and the arrival or handoff. A stationary image held
for its entire shot, or the same mild fade/zoom applied to every asset, does not
meet this direction. Reading holds and deliberately flat before-states can be
still when their contrast serves the approved sequence; constant movement is not
the goal.

Choose by subject and available material: a cutout can cross the frame and resolve
into a close-up; separated photo layers can create a depth move; a mask can reveal
a decisive detail; an interface can demonstrate an actual interaction; a modeled
object can turn through light. Scale contrast, acceleration, framing, and sound
can create impact without rotating everything or applying every effect. Treat the
reference film as an intensity and clarity benchmark, not a required palette,
product, rotation angle, effect list, or tempo.

Check that the asset can support the promised motion. A still PNG supports image
transforms, not a genuine product turn exposing new surfaces. For a real turn,
obtain or build a 3D model, or use suitable multi-view rendered footage. State
which method is planned and which has actually been implemented. Validate a
short motion study before relying on a complex hero move in the final sequence.

Connect shots through a persistent feature: position, shape, word, color, motion
direction, or rhythm. Examples include a circle becoming a product detail, a
selection boundary becoming a device frame, or a camera move retaining the same
text across two surfaces. A true morph needs correspondence between shapes;
a crossfade alone should not be described as one.

Give the strongest transformation a setup and payoff. Keep surrounding moments
clear enough that the surprise can register. Avoid assembling unrelated effects
solely to increase their count.

At 30 FPS, an accent might start around 6–12 frames, a reveal around 12–24 frames,
and a simple phrase hold around 30–60 frames. These are provisional iteration
ranges, not defaults to force onto copy. Adjust for length, language, action, and
music, and re-evaluate at normal speed. Preserve seconds when changing FPS.

## Color, surface, and light

Let the scene hierarchy determine contrast. Black or white can isolate a vivid
subject; gradients, luminous color, warm environments, and tactile materials can
also belong. Select a coherent palette and lighting logic for the subject.
Use blur, glow, depth, reflections, and grain where they explain energy, material,
or focus. Evaluate their readability during motion and in the settled frame.

## Sound and editing share the same events

Choose a sonic character with the visual direction: precise, tactile, playful,
warm, spacious, or energetic. Decide the roles of music, designed effects, voice,
and silence before detailed animation. Build a small related palette with enough
variation to avoid repeated stock accents.

Anchor cues to meaningful events, not automatically to layer starts. A sweep may
anticipate a reveal; a transient may mark contact or arrival; a tail can carry
continuity into the next shot. Silence and reduced energy can make the next
accent feel larger. Effects need not accompany every object or every cut.

Possible pairings to audition, not prescriptions:

| Visual gesture | Sonic proposal | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Giant word lands | Short low body with a crisp onset | Contact feels aligned; the sound does not overwhelm the phrase |
| Mask opens | Filtered texture or rising musical note | Arrival meets the reveal instead of trailing accidentally |
| Surface detail transforms | Related timbres changing character | The sound makes the material change feel coherent |
| Brief kinetic burst | A few related rhythmic accents | Accents reinforce the hierarchy rather than every letter |
| Final lockup settles | Harmonic resolution and a controlled tail | Identity stays readable through the ending |

Music may carry an action normally assigned to an effect, and an effect may
contribute pitch or rhythm. Use musical phrases, changes in texture, pauses, and
selected beats to organize the edit. Leave reading holds where comprehension
needs them instead of forcing all cuts onto beats.

Balance voice intelligibility, musical energy, and effects in the actual mix.
Audition the result at ordinary playback level and, when possible, on headphones
and small speakers. Use measurements for peaks and delivery targets alongside
listening, with suitable headroom and intentional audio boundaries.

## Editorial review before showing the user

Challenge the preview as a viewer who has not read the brief:

- Does each phrase sell or explain something? Replace empty copy, and clarify
  ambiguous technical imagery with a legible excerpt, a meaningful headline, or
  its visible result. If presenting code as the video's source, use actual source.
- Is the intended focal point dominant at each beat? Separate competing gestures
  in time; remove unexplained lines, panels, and secondary copy.
- Do hero words perform, and do images meet the motion criteria above? Inspect
  an action frame and its resolved state; a polished still alone proves neither.
- Does the product have enough identity/context to be understood? Add its name
  where useful without competing with the reveal.
- Does the soundtrack deliver the agreed energy and support the gestures? Rework
  repetitive generic accents or mismatched pacing; faster is a choice, not a rule.
- Does the ending communicate one clear action with a readable final hold?

When feedback exposes a failure, record the reason and intended correction in the
production plan, apply it to comparable beats in that film, and verify the revised
output. Ask about a genuinely new preference; resolve execution weaknesses within
existing authorization. Carry forward the principle rather than making that
video's exact correction mandatory for every future subject.
